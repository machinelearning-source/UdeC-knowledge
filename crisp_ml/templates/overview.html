KnowBot: a Retrieval-Augmented Generation assistant for the institutional regulations and the academic calendar of the University of Cundinamarca

KnowBot answers students' questions about the official documents of the university with verifiable citations. The project developed the chatbot, a Machine Learning re-ranker built with the six CRISP-ML phases, and KnowBot Desk, a private web information system that manages academic inquiries and uses the model inside that process.

Team: Oscar Javier Bello Daza, Mariana León Triana, Luis Orlando Ramírez Mosquera, Luis Alberto Rebolledo Ariza.

1. Project title

KnowBot: a Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) conversational assistant for querying the institutional regulations and the academic calendar of the University of Cundinamarca.

The approved title is kept. During development the scope grew with two components that serve the same purpose: a learned re-ranker that orders the retrieved sources (Activity 1) and KnowBot Desk, the web information system that manages the inquiries that need a reviewed answer (Activities 2 and 3).

2. Problem statement and justification

Problem. The information students need for academic procedures is spread across long documents: the Undergraduate Student Regulations, Agreement 005 of 2025, the report of the Student Regulations Commission and the 2026 Academic Calendar. Finding a requirement, a deadline or a date means reading dozens of pages, and the calendar is a wide table with one column per month that is hard to read on a screen or a phone.

Affected population and consequences. Undergraduate students repeat the same questions to program coordinations and offices, misread requirements or miss dates (course homologation, semester cancellation, voluntary withdrawal, registration dates). Coordinators and advisors spend time answering repeated questions without a record of what was asked and answered. Keyword search does not solve it, because students phrase questions with words different from the documents.

Evidence. On 100 labeled validation questions, the first fragment retrieved by vector search came from the page that answers the question in only 46% of the cases, and 6 questions had no fragment of the right page among the candidates; retrieval, not text generation, was the main cause of wrong or "not found" answers.

Guiding question. How can a conversational assistant based on Machine Learning answer students' questions about the regulations and the calendar accurately, with verifiable sources, and hand the cases that need a person to an advisor with a record of the decision?

Justification. Language models answer in natural language but can invent information. Retrieval-Augmented Generation reduces that risk by grounding each answer in the official fragments and citing the document and page. A learned re-ranker can put the page that answers the question first, and an inquiry management system turns the model's suggestion into a supervised decision: the advisor confirms or corrects the page, and every confirmation becomes new labeled data. The project integrates Machine Learning, natural language processing and software engineering, is aligned with the research line Learning, knowledge, technologies, communication and digitalization (Agreement 09 of 2021) and the Digital Transmodern Educational Model (MEDIT), and is replicable in other institutions.

4. Reference framework

  • Retrieval-Augmented Generation. Lewis et al. (2020) combine a document retriever with a generative model; the answer is built from retrieved fragments, which reduces hallucinations and allows citing the source. KnowBot applies it to institutional documents.
  • Sentence embeddings. Sentence-BERT (Reimers & Gurevych, 2019) and its multilingual extension (Reimers & Gurevych, 2020) place Spanish and English sentences in the same vector space. KnowBot uses paraphrase-multilingual-MiniLM-L12-v2 locally with FastEmbed, because questions may be in English and the documents are in Spanish.
  • Similarity search and diversity. FAISS (Johnson et al., 2021) finds the nearest vectors quickly; Maximal Marginal Relevance (Carbonell & Goldstein, 1998) balances relevance and diversity of the retrieved fragments.
  • Keyword ranking. BM25 (Robertson & Zaragoza, 2009) scores exact terms that embeddings blur, such as official names and article numbers; it is combined with vector search.
  • Learning to rank. Ranking can be learned as pointwise classification of (query, document) pairs (Liu, 2009). The re-ranker predicts the probability that a fragment comes from the page that answers the question.
  • Random Forest. An ensemble of decision trees trained on bootstrap samples (Breiman, 2001), robust to noisy labels and able to capture feature interactions; implemented with scikit-learn (Pedregosa et al., 2011).
  • Evaluation of RAG systems. Retrieval is measured with MRR and Hit@k; answers can be judged by a language model (Zheng et al., 2023), as systematized by RAGAS (Es et al., 2024).
  • CRISP-ML(Q). A process model with quality assurance for Machine Learning projects in six phases, from business understanding to monitoring (Studer et al., 2021). It structures the re-ranker project.
  • CDIO. Conceive, Design, Implement, Operate (Crawley et al., 2014), the engineering approach suggested by the Faculty guidelines, used for the overall project plan.

5. Methodology and development schedule

The overall project followed CDIO (conceive, design, implement, operate). The Machine Learning component followed the six CRISP-ML phases, each documented in this application with its evidence:

CRISP-ML phaseWhat was doneEvidence
1. Business and data understandingProblem, stakeholders, business and ML objectives, success criteria fixed before modeling, data quality of 100 questions and 290 fragments.Phase 1 page, data_quality.json
2. Data engineeringCandidate generation (25 vector, 15 BM25, exact matches), 23 features, cleaning, scaling inside the pipeline, split by question to prevent leakage.build_dataset.py, dataset.csv, split.json
3. Model engineeringThree algorithms, grid search with grouped 5-fold cross-validation, three recorded feature experiments.train.py, cv_summary.csv, experiments/
4. Model evaluationRandom Forest selected (CV MRR 0.594 against 0.585); test MRR 0.658 against 0.724; team assessment of the 9 errors; decision: shadow mode.test_metrics.json, team_review.json, figures
5. Model deploymentFlask application on Render, API and demo, slim embedding mode to fit 512 MB, functional test on the public URL; integration into KnowBot Desk.render.yaml, functional_test.json
6. Monitoring and maintenanceSeven indicators with thresholds, PSI drift check, inference history with model version, prediction compared with the advisor's confirmed page, retraining criteria.monitoring.py, Desk reports

Web information system. KnowBot Desk extends the Flask application with a persistent database (PostgreSQL in production, SQLite locally), registration and login with hashed passwords, three roles (Student, Advisor, Coordinator) enforced on the server, the inquiry workflow (create, model suggestion, escalation, answer with a confirmed source, rating and closing), user and category management, reports, an audit log and the inference history. The model routes each inquiry: below a probability of 0.30 it is escalated to the coordinator; otherwise it goes to the advisors with the suggested page.
